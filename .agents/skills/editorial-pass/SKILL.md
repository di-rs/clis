# Editorial pass

Read the whole document before editing individual sentences. Identify its central
message and the reader's task. Review structure before paragraphs and paragraphs
before wording; polishing a misplaced section does not fix its placement.

## Review the reader's path

Read once for understanding and again for diagnosis. Check whether the opening
sets useful context, concepts appear before they are needed, and each section
advances the page's purpose. Skim headings and paragraph openings to expose gaps
or repeated introductions.

Compare related pages as a reading journey. Keep detailed contracts in one useful
home, retaining enough local context for a page to be usable. Consolidate fragments
when readers must repeatedly switch pages to finish one task. Length alone is not
a defect; preserve mechanisms, limitations, and recovery detail that readers need.

Check terminology and internal contradictions. Technical claims still need the
[technical-documentation](../technical-documentation/SKILL.md) review; an editorial
pass must not silently change product behavior or settle an open design choice.

## Remove noise without removing meaning

- Remove investigation narration, such as who checked a source, unless provenance
  itself matters to the claim. Keep methods when explaining measurements.
- Remove abandoned alternatives and resolved questions unless they prevent a
  plausible mistake or explain a consequential decision.
- Remove drafting scaffolding, repeated summaries, and claims about the document
  itself. Preserve genuine unresolved decisions explicitly.
- Shorten background that readers only need to accept; retain context they need
  to judge a tradeoff or perform an operation safely.

Explain jargon at first use and unpack dense causal chains. Prefer connected prose
for relationships and tables or lists for genuinely parallel material. Use visuals
when they help explain the subject, not to satisfy an illustration quota.

## Apply within the request

For an authorized writing or editing task, form a brief diagnosis and apply the
supported fixes without another approval round. The diagnosis can remain working
notes; do not create an extra memo file unless requested.

For review-only requests, report the central message, dominant problems, and a few
representative examples with concrete revisions; leave the source unchanged.
Ask only when unresolved meaning or a consequential author decision affects
correctness. Continue independent fixes while that question is open.

After editing, recheck changed claims and links, then read the complete result.
The final prose must stand alone for a reader who never saw the conversation.
